\begin{abstract}
Lightweight per-frame object detectors can localize visible driver cues for a downstream monitoring pipeline, but comparisons are easily confounded by identity leakage, inconsistent optimization, best-run selection, and incomparable timing boundaries. DMS-Eval is a controlled benchmark of YOLO11n, YOLO26n, and D-FINE-N on 15,723 manually annotated $640\times640$ RGB crops derived from 68 public Driver Monitoring Dataset source sessions and 14 subjects. Four foreground cues and an implicit no-cue outcome form a five-outcome frame ontology; 12,722 frames contain no target box. The fixed 8/3/3 subject split, three predeclared seeds, complete nine-run retention, validation-only selection, frozen manifests, single protected test pass per run, shared COCO evaluator, and mean $\pm$ sample-standard-deviation reporting prohibit best-run selection. Training standardizes shared fairness controls while retaining pinned architecture-specific official recipes and a closed adaptation list. Batch-1 model-only and tensor-to-final-detection latency are measured separately on one RTX 4060. \AbstractResultSentence\ The benchmark concerns configured per-frame detector systems; it does not validate temporal state inference, alert logic, clinical safety, or real-world deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Vision-based driver monitoring spans observable behavior, inferred driver state, and intervention logic. Drowsiness and distraction research commonly combines facial cues, vehicle signals, or physiology over time \cite{ramzan2019survey}. A single-frame detector addresses a narrower layer: it localizes visible objects or facial configurations that a temporal system may later aggregate. This distinction matters because a yawn-shaped mouth in one frame is not longitudinal fatigue evidence, and a detected phone does not by itself specify risk or an alert policy.

Published driver-behavior comparisons often emphasize whole-frame classification. The AUC distracted-driver work \cite{eraqi2019driver} and the CNN-versus-transformer benchmark of Koay \textit{et al.} \cite{koay2021benchmark} report posture categories, while Drive\&Act \cite{martin2019driveact} and UTA-RLDD \cite{ghoddoosian2019rldd} support fine-grained or temporal behavior recognition. These tasks are valuable but do not provide a controlled spatial comparison of compact CNN and transformer detector systems. Frame-level random splits can also place the same person's appearance in training and test data, weakening claims about unseen-driver generalization.

DMS-Eval therefore asks: \textit{under fixed subject-disjoint data and shared hardware, how do three lightweight configured detector systems compare in localization quality, operating-point behavior, latency, and resources?} It contributes:
\begin{itemize}
\item a DMD-derived \cite{ortega2020dmd} four-foreground-class annotation set with an explicit no-cue frame outcome, a fixed $640\times640$ crop, and a deterministic 8/3/3 subject-disjoint split;
\item a predeclared fairness protocol with three common seeds, all nine runs retained, validation-only checkpoint and threshold selection, frozen manifests, one protected test pass per run, and no best-run selection; and
\item a shared evaluation and deployment-focused report separating accuracy, native postprocessing latency, throughput, parameters, dual FLOP estimates, peak memory, and standardized FP16 model size.
\end{itemize}

The contribution is a controlled benchmark, not a novel detector architecture or a complete driver-monitoring system. Temporal inference, driver-state aggregation, alert generation, and on-road safety validation remain outside scope.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Driver Monitoring Data and Tasks}
DMD provides synchronized RGB, depth, and infrared recordings of driver face, body, and hands across distraction, gaze, and drowsiness scenarios \cite{ortega2020dmd}. DMS-Eval adapts only public driver-facing RGB material. Unlike full-frame action classification \cite{eraqi2019driver,koay2021benchmark} or sequence modeling \cite{martin2019driveact,ghoddoosian2019rldd}, it formulates four visible cues as bounding-box detection targets and retains natural no-cue frames to exercise false-positive suppression.

\subsection{Lightweight CNN and Transformer Detectors}
YOLO11 is an Ultralytics real-time detector family without a formal paper; the official software citation is used \cite{yolo11_ultralytics}. YOLO26 introduces a native one-to-one end-to-end path with NMS-free default inference and a revised training recipe \cite{jocher2026yolo26}. DETR established direct set prediction \cite{carion2020detr}, RT-DETR made the paradigm practical for real-time operation \cite{zhao2024rtdetr}, and D-FINE refined distributional box regression and localization self-distillation \cite{peng2025dfine}. Cross-paper COCO numbers cannot answer the present question because datasets, training budgets, precision, postprocessing, and hardware differ.

\subsection{Evaluation Rigor}
System benchmarking requires explicit workload, hardware, precision, and latency boundaries \cite{reddi2020mlperf}. DMS-Eval treats native postprocessing as part of the deployed detector path, reports individual seeds and sample variability, and prevents test results from influencing selection. These controls support a configured-system comparison; they do not isolate architecture as a causal factor.

\section{Dataset Adaptation and Annotation}
\subsection{Source, Sampling, and Fixed Crop}
The DMD collection describes 81 full study recordings; the frozen public manifest used here contains 68 source-video entries across 14 subjects and the distraction, drowsiness, and gaze subsets. Frames were uniformly sampled at 1~FPS from $1280\times720$ RGB face streams. Every stored image is the same direct crop $(x,y,w,h)=(272,71,640,640)$; no resize, letterbox padding, or aspect-ratio deformation is applied. The pipeline yielded 15,723 images. The fixed spatial window reduces irrelevant cabin content while making preprocessing identical for every model.

\subsection{Five-Outcome Frame Ontology}
Four foreground object classes are trained and evaluated: \texttt{yawning}, \texttt{hand\_over\_mouth}, \texttt{drinking}, and \texttt{phone\_use}. The fifth frame-level outcome is \textit{no cue/background}; it is represented by an empty annotation rather than a detector class. This distinction preserves correct object-detection semantics. Each frame has zero or one box. A visible yawn with a hand occluding the mouth is labeled only \texttt{hand\_over\_mouth}; the two labels are mutually exclusive.

Annotations were produced by full manual frame inspection in Label Studio \cite{labelstudio}, converted to one authoritative COCO file, clamped to image bounds, and checked for the one-box maximum and frozen counts. Boxes enclose the mouth aperture for \texttt{yawning}, the head/face and occluding hand for \texttt{hand\_over\_mouth}, the container and interacting hand for \texttt{drinking}, and the phone plus interacting hand in calling posture for \texttt{phone\_use}. There are 3,001 positive frames and 12,722 no-cue frames (80.91\%).

\subsection{Subject-Disjoint 8/3/3 Split}
The 14 subjects are assigned once to train, validation, or test (Table~\ref{tab:split}); intersections are empty. All $\binom{14}{8}\binom{6}{3}=60{,}060$ ordered assignments were scored by the worst absolute relative deviation, then overall root-mean-square deviation, test worst deviation, test root-mean-square deviation, and lexicographic subject order. The objective covers positive-frame rate and the four foreground proportions in every split. The selected split has 5.4812\% worst relative deviation. Training subjects are 01, 04, 06--09, 13, and 14; validation subjects are 02, 03, and 11; test subjects are 05, 10, and 12. Only training order is deterministically shuffled (seed 13); validation and test order remain native.

\section{Controlled Benchmark Protocol}
\subsection{Models and Fairness Policy}
The candidates are YOLO11n, YOLO26n, and D-FINE-N. All receive the same images, four foreground classes, $640\times640$ inputs, 220 epochs, physical batch 8, fixed four-step accumulation from the first batch (effective batch 32), no early stopping, \texttt{drop\_last=false}, sample-correct incomplete accumulation windows, CUDA automatic mixed precision (AMP), seeds 13/37/73, and the same RTX 4060.

Fairness controls are shared, whereas architecture-specific optimization and augmentation follow pinned official recipes. The closed adaptations are only: DMS-Eval data/classes, $640\times640$ input, batch 8, accumulation 4, 220 epochs, the three seeds, and disabled early stopping. No model receives bespoke tuning. Thus, different pinned recipes do not violate the protocol; equal epochs mean equal data exposure, not equal FLOPs, wall time, or capacity. Ultralytics 8.4.123 uses its pinned \texttt{optimizer=auto} recipe and package augmentations. D-FINE uses the pinned commit \texttt{956d170}, AdamW, base/backbone learning rates 0.0008/0.0004, weight decay 0.0001, and a predefined epoch-148 augmentation/EMA transition without validation-guided state reload.

\subsection{Validation-Only Selection and Protected Test Access}
Every retained epoch is exported on validation data. For each model-seed run, validation mAP@0.5:0.95 selects the checkpoint; ties use mAP@0.5 and then the later epoch. The selected predictions then calibrate confidence $\tau\in\{0.01,\ldots,0.99\}$ by maximum micro-F1, with higher precision and higher $\tau$ as ties. Validation never alters the training trajectory.

The checkpoint, prediction, calibration, and hashes form an immutable model-seed manifest. A suite must contain exactly the three models crossed with the three seeds before any protected access. Each manifest receives one append-only-ledger-gated test pass. Test annotations and images cannot guide checkpoint, threshold, recipe, run retention, or prose. Historical \texttt{*\_backup\_epoch120} folders are excluded from the official matrix.

\subsection{Shared Metrics and Aggregation}
All adapters emit COCO-style predictions, and one pycocotools implementation \cite{lin2014coco} computes mAP@0.5:0.95, mAP@0.5, and per-class AP. At the validation-calibrated threshold, same-class detections are greedily matched in descending score at IoU $\geq0.50$. Precision, recall, and micro-F1 are
\begin{equation}
P=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\quad R=\frac{TP}{TP+FN},\quad F_1=\frac{2PR}{P+R}.
\end{equation}
False alarms are $100$ times the false-positive detections on the 2,599 negative test frames divided by 2,599; this rate may exceed 100. For each model, all three seed values $x_i$ are retained and summarized by
\begin{equation}
\bar{x}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_i,\qquad s=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{3}(x_i-\bar{x})^2}{3-1}}.
\end{equation}
No best seed is selected and no significance claim is inferred from three runs.

\subsection{Latency, Resources, and Environment}
The protected environment is Python 3.12.10, PyTorch 2.4.1+cu121, CUDA 12.1, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 8~GB. Models and inputs remain FP32 in storage; forward execution uses CUDA autocast FP16. With batch 1 and input $1\times3\times640\times640$, ten untimed full-path warm-ups precede measurement. Synchronized CUDA events measure preprocessed tensor to raw model output. A synchronized high-resolution wall clock measures preprocessed tensor to normalized final detections, including architecture-required decoding and YOLO11n NMS. Disk I/O, decode, preprocessing, metrics, and serialization are excluded. Both boundaries report p50/p95/p99 and sustained FPS.

The same pass records peak allocated VRAM, parameters, THOP ($2\times$ MACs) and PyTorch-profiler FLOP estimates, and an inference-only FP16 state-dictionary size. FLOP values are tool- and operator-coverage-dependent. Exact package, source-recipe, weight, annotation, split, checkpoint, manifest, suite, result, and generated-asset hashes support reproduction.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-offs}
\DiscussionTradeoffSentence\ Interpretation will compare configured systems rather than attribute differences to architecture alone. The primary evidence consists of mean quality, class behavior, negative-frame false alarms, native tensor-to-detection latency, throughput, parameters, FLOPs, VRAM, and standardized size. Forward-only latency is diagnostic; tensor-to-final-detections latency is the deployment-facing comparison within the stated boundary.

\subsection{Qualitative Failure Analysis}
The protected evaluator predefines correct detection, negative-frame false positive, false negative, class confusion, and localization error categories. It deterministically ranks three examples per category during the single test pass. A publication montage may use the predeclared reference seed 13 for illustration, while numerical conclusions use all seeds. No later image search or second test traversal is permitted.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
The data cover 14 subjects and a fixed public DMD subset; rare cues are imbalanced and camera placement, illumination, demographics, and domains remain limited. The subject split prevents identity overlap but does not establish cross-dataset generalization. Three seeds characterize only limited stochastic variation. Common AMP and data exposure do not equalize optimization dynamics, compute, or postprocessing. RTX 4060 latency is not embedded-platform latency, and the timing boundary excludes capture, decode, preprocessing, temporal aggregation, and application logic.

Future work should add external subject-disjoint datasets, night and occlusion stress tests, calibration and robustness analysis, and direct profiling on target automotive hardware. More importantly, it should evaluate temporal cue aggregation, longitudinal drowsiness or distraction inference, uncertainty-aware driver-state estimation, alert logic, human factors, and controlled real-world safety outcomes. Those layers must not be inferred from the present per-frame detector benchmark.

\section{Conclusion}
DMS-Eval fixes the dataset adaptation, five-outcome frame ontology, subject-disjoint split, shared controls, pinned recipe policy, three seeds, validation-only selection, protected test access, metric implementation, timing boundaries, and reproducibility artifacts before final testing. \ConclusionResultSentence\ The result is evidence about three configured lightweight per-frame detector systems, not a novel architecture, temporal fatigue estimator, alerting system, clinical claim, or deployment validation.
